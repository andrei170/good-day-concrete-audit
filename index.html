Three Things On Your Site That Are Not Yours
One — the offer is written for a car wash
gooddayconcrete.net · homepage "Big Savings: 10% OFF Any Detail Service of $99 and Up"

"Detail service" is auto-detailing language. Concrete contractors do not sell detail services. This is template copy for a different trade that was never replaced.

It is also the wrong offer regardless. Ten percent off a ninety-nine dollar job is ten dollars. Your actual work is driveways, foundations and patios worth thousands. The one promotional message on your site is aimed at a customer you do not want.

Two — two different founding dates, side by side

Your site displays "Since 2010" and "25 Years of Experience" as badges next to each other. 2010 was sixteen years ago.

Both can be true if you personally have twenty-five years in concrete and founded the company in 2010 — but the site does not say that, so a customer reads a business that cannot keep its own story straight. Pick one and explain it.

Three — the gallery cannot be verified as your work

Every photograph is named sequentially — gallery1.jpg through gallery19.jpg — and stored in the shared template folder rather than uploaded individually. That is the naming pattern of images supplied with the template.

We cannot tell from the outside whether these are your pours or platform stock. A customer cannot tell either, and that is the problem. If they are your jobs they need captions, locations and dates. If they are not, they need replacing with photos from your phone.

On The CRM Question You Asked
The honest sequence

A CRM is worth having, but it solves the problem after the enquiry arrives — chasing quotes, following up, stopping jobs falling through the cracks. It does not create enquiries.

Right now your form asks nothing, so a CRM would be catching leads containing a name and a sentence. Fix the form first and the CRM has something worth managing. Ask for job type, approximate square footage, the address and the timeline, and every enquiry arrives already priceable.

Then the CRM does the part you actually want: automatic reply within seconds, follow-up if you have not quoted in two days, and a record of every job you quoted and lost so you can go back to them next season.

You already have the measurement layer installed, which is the piece most contractors are missing and the piece that makes everything else provable.
